## Confirmed
- Layout, from OSM and the photos:
  - The hall faces south-south-east toward Sư Liễu Quán.
  - The gate is on the hall's axis.
  - The stupa is on the left of the yard and the Bodhi tree on the right.
  - The two-storey hall stands on the east of the yard.
  - All the outlines come from OSM.
- Tam quan, built 1965:
  - Three openings, the middle one wider and higher.
  - Grey piers edged in ochre and a green name board.
  - Small tiled roofs on brackets and black iron leaves.
- Street wall: cream bays with grey key-fret panels between pilasters, on a dark stone base. The yard stands above the street.
- Chánh điện, rebuilt 2006–2010:
  - Two levels in the "trùng thiềm điệp ốc" style. Footprint 42 × 35.9 m (Wikipedia); OSM gives about 24 × 38 m plus the wings.
  - Grey granite podium with a stair across the front.
  - Red-brown columns round a verandah and cream walls.
  - Terracotta two-tier roofs with a painted panel band between the tiers and white ceramic dragons on the ridges.
- Bell and drum towers either side of the hall.
- Tháp Ấn Tôn:
  - Octagonal, seven storeys, about 27 m.
  - Red-brown brick with cream balustrades and brown tiled eaves on every storey.
  - Ringed by slender Ashoka trees.
- Bodhi tree, planted 1939, in the front yard on the right. Red-brick forecourt.

## Street View check (2026)
Compared from car panoramas on Điện Biên Phủ and a visitor panorama in the yard. These are visual references only; nothing was traced from them.
- The site sits on a rubble-stone base about 1.3 m high along Điện Biên Phủ. The yard is drawn 1.4 m above the highest ground at the wall.
- The street wall is cream, with a band of dark key-fret openings, square posts with ball finials, and a brown coping.
- The front hall has tall red columns, a low lower roof, a clerestory of grey-green panels and a pale ceramic ridge crest with dragons at the ends. Its tiles are a dull red-brown.
- The towers have a tall lower storey with large lattice windows. From the street the drum tower's top reaches about the hall's hip crest; from the yard the wing looks lower. The model follows the street view.
- Not modelled: the hexagonal red-brick paving and grey concrete strip along the east hall, which has two storeys plus a pent roof and balcony; the Ashoka trees in planters in the yard.
